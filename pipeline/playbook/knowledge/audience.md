# Audience

## Who Buys This Book

**Primary buyer:** Parent (mom, ~80% of purchases), grandparent, aunt/uncle
**Recipient:** The parent of the child, on behalf of the child
**End reader:** The child (age 0–3) — but they cannot read. The parent reads TO them.

The child is almost always a "vegetable" — 1–2 years old, completely passive.
The book is not FOR the child to enjoy alone. It is for the parent to feel seen and loved.

---

## Why They Buy

1. **They want the child to feel special** — a book with their name and face archetype feels magical
2. **They want to remember this time** — keepsake value, something to keep forever
3. **They want to give a meaningful gift** — not another toy; something emotional
4. **Occasion-driven** — 1st birthday, baby shower, baptism, Christmas, naming day

The product sells an emotion: *"Your child is unique, and this book proves it."*

---

## Emotional Triggers (Use These in Text)

- Warmth and safety ("wrapped in love", "safe and small")
- Wonder and discovery ("the world is new", "every day a gift")
- Pride ("look how far you've come", "you did it")
- Unconditional love ("always and forever", "no matter what")
- Belonging ("you are ours", "you are home")

**Avoid:** adventure without resolution, conflict without comfort, any sadness.
Every emotional low must immediately resolve into warmth.

---

## Tone Calibration

The buyer is:
- Emotional and sentimental
- Not reading critically — they scan and feel
- Easily moved by their child's name appearing on the page
- Likely to cry (good cry) if the finale lands well

Write for the parent reading aloud at bedtime, voice cracking slightly on the last page.

---

## What "Personalized" Means to the Buyer

They expect:
1. Child's **name** woven into the story (not just on the cover)
2. A character that **looks like** their child (skin tone, hair — via archetype selection)
3. The feeling that this book **could not exist without their child**

They do NOT expect a fully custom plot. The formula is invisible to them.
