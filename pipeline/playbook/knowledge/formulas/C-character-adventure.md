# Formula C — Character Adventure

## Concept

The child IS the main character — usually represented as an animal (goose, bear, bunny, fox).
The book follows one day or one short adventure: morning → discovery → small mishap → warmth.
This is the only formula with a real beginning, middle, and end.

The emotional core: *"You are playful and wild and perfectly, wonderfully you."*

---

## Best For

- "Silly [Animal] Book for Baby"
- Baby shower gifts (lighter tone)
- 1st birthday with a character theme
- Any listing with a playful, funny title

---

## Tone

Bouncy, playful, a little chaotic. The reading rhythm should feel like a child bouncing.
The parent reads with fun voices. There should be at least one moment that makes them smile.

---

## Three Subtypes

### C1 — Curious Explorer
The character discovers the natural world: bugs, flowers, ponds, hills.
Each spread = a new discovery. Tone is wonder-driven.
Best animal: bear cub, hedgehog, small bunny.

### C2 — Silly Troublemaker ← (default for "Silly Goose" type listings)
The character causes gentle chaos: spills, splashing, chasing things.
Each spread = a small mishap + joyful reaction. Tone is comedic.
Best animal: goose, duckling, puppy.

### C3 — Brave Little One
The character faces something slightly scary and overcomes it.
Each spread = one small fear → one small act of courage.
Tone is gently empowering.
Best animal: lion cub, little bear, small wolf pup.

---

## Narrative Arc (Spreads 01–15 + Afterword)

Do not use a hardcoded list of scenes. Instead, invent a unique, cohesive adventure for the character that fits the specific listing or theme. The adventure must have a clear beginning, middle, and end.

- **Spread 01**: Dedication (Static)
- **Spreads 02–04 (The Start/Morning)**: The character wakes up or starts their day full of energy. Introduce the tone (curiosity, silliness, or bravery).
- **Spreads 05–11 (The Adventure & Mishaps)**: The core of the book. The character explores, causes gentle chaos, or faces small fears. Invent specific, visually distinct actions for each spread (e.g., chasing a butterfly, splashing in a puddle, getting slightly muddy, finding a new friend). Ensure pacing goes up and down.
- **Spreads 12–14 (Resolution & Winding Down)**: The adventure concludes. A gentle rescue, a shared meal, or returning home tired. The final spread (14) must bring the character to rest or sleep.
- **Spread 15**: Afterword/Finale (Static: "You are playful and wild and perfectly you.")

**Crucial constraint for Agent 2**: You must invent your own specific adventure scenes. Do not copy the generic ideas above if the listing provides a better aesthetic (e.g., if the listing is "Space Bear", invent a zero-gravity mishap, not a mud puddle). Ensure the adventure moves forward without stalling.
---

## Text Pattern Per Spread

```
[Action line — what the character did]
[Sensory detail — what it felt/looked/sounded like]
[Reaction — joy, surprise, determination]
[{Name} line — connects child to the character]
```

Example (Silly Goose, puddle spread):
```
A muddy little puddle caught
the goose's curious eye.
She jumped right in and splattered —
mud went up to the sky!

That's just like you, dear {Name},
the world's most joyful mess.
```

Note: the "{Name}" line can be a brief bridge verse at the end of the spread,
or integrated into the main quatrain. Both work.

---

## Visual Notes

- The animal character IS visually the "child archetype" — same hair color, eye color
- The character should have one consistent outfit/color throughout (signals identity)
- Action spreads dominate (8–9 of the 14 story spreads)
- Quiet spreads: meals, being held by a parent-animal, falling asleep
- Backgrounds change every spread — this is the formula with the most varied environments
- Parent-animal (if shown) should be warm and background, never dominant
