# Formula A — Milestone Journey

## Concept

The book is a celebration of the child's first year. Each spread is one milestone —
a moment the parents recognize and feel emotionally connected to.
There is no dramatic plot. The arc is time: from newborn to one-year-old.

The emotional core: *"Look how far you've come. Look how loved you are."*

---

## Best For

- 1st Birthday keepsake books
- "Baby's First Year" books
- "Daddy/Mommy and Me" (modified version — see below)

---

## Tone

Warm, nostalgic, celebratory. Like a parent narrating a photo album out loud.
The reader (parent) should feel proud and a little emotional.

---

## Narrative Arc (Spreads 01–15 + Afterword)

Do not use a hardcoded list of scenes. Instead, invent unique milestones and moments that fit the specific listing or theme, following this emotional arc (from birth/arrival to the present day).

- **Spread 01**: Dedication (Static)
- **Spreads 02–05 (The Arrival & Early Days)**: Focus on firsts that happen when the baby is very small and immobile (e.g., first meeting, first smile, first bath, first time being held).
- **Spreads 06–10 (Discovering the World & Movement)**: Focus on active exploration (e.g., first roll, first giggle, messy eating, crawling, discovering a favorite toy or pet).
- **Spreads 11–13 (Becoming a Toddler)**: Focus on bigger, more complex actions (e.g., first steps, first words, playing a game together).
- **Spread 14 (The Close/Bedtime)**: A quiet, winding-down moment (e.g., bedtime story, rocking to sleep).
- **Spread 15**: Afterword/Finale (Static: "You are one, and oh, how loved you are.")

**Crucial constraint for Agent 2**: The examples above (first bath, first steps) are just *ideas*. You must invent your own specific scenes that fit the exact theme of the book. Ensure a progression from newborn to one-year-old.
---

## Flexibility Notes

- Not all milestones need to be included. Swap any scene for a more relevant one.
- The order is approximate — Spreads 02–14 can be rearranged for pacing.
- Spreads 13–14 (birthday + sleep) should always stay near the end.

---

## Text Pattern Per Spread

```
[Active verb describing the milestone],
[Sensory or emotional detail],
[Parent's reaction or the child's feeling],
[Line with {Name}]
```

Example (First Steps):
```
Wiggle, wobble, one small step,
{Name} laughed and tried again.
Up you rose on tiny feet —
the whole world clapped, again, again.
```

---

## Modified Version: "Daddy and Me / Mommy and Me"

Follow the same narrative arc structure (Spreads 02–14), but replace solo child milestones with **shared moments** between the child and the parent. 

Instead of generating generic milestones, invent unique scenes such as:
- The day we first met
- Our first walk outside
- Reading together
- Bathtime splashes
- Dancing in the kitchen
- Laughing until we couldn't stop
- Watching the stars together

**Constraint**: Distribute these shared moments across the arc, moving from quiet early days to active play, ending with a shared quiet bedtime moment. Do not hardcode a specific sequence; invent the best sequence for the book's specific theme.
---

## Visual Notes

- Every spread should feel like a memory photo come to life
- Active spreads: baby in motion (crawling, stepping, reaching)
- Quiet spreads: baby being held, sleeping, nestled
- Alternate active/quiet throughout
- Consistent character archetype across all 14 story spreads (Spreads 02–15)
