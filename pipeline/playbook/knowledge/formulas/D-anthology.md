# Formula D — Anthology

## Concept

A collection of short, standalone stories connected by a theme and a guide character.
Each spread is its own mini-story with its own setting and characters.
The child ({Name}) appears at the end of each story as the moral recipient.

The emotional core: *"These stories are yours, {Name}. Their lessons belong to you."*

---

## Best For

- Bible storybook listings (non-ceremony context)
- "Bedtime stories" compilations
- Listings emphasizing educational or faith-based content
- Multi-story gift books

---

## Tone

Calm, gentle, slightly educational. The parent reads one story per night.
Each spread should feel complete and satisfying on its own.

---

## Structure

Unlike other formulas, this one uses spreads less like a film and more like book chapters.
Some stories may take 2 spreads if the scene is complex. Budget accordingly.

Typical structure for a 15-spread Bible anthology:
```
Spread 01 — Dedication
Spread 02 — Introduction: guide character appears (e.g., a bunny with a lantern)
Spread 03–04 — Story 1 (2 spreads)
Spread 05–06 — Story 2 (2 spreads)
Spread 07–08 — Story 3 (2 spreads)
Spread 09–10 — Story 4 (2 spreads)
Spread 11–12 — Story 5 (2 spreads)
Spread 13–14 — Story 6 (2 spreads)
Spread 15 — Finale: guide character tucks the child in, "all these stories are yours"
```

---

## Narrative Arc (Spreads 01–15 + Afterword)

Do not use a hardcoded list of stories. Instead, select or invent 6 mini-stories that fit the specific listing or theme perfectly.

- **Spread 01**: Dedication (Static)
- **Spread 02**: Introduction of the guide character setting the stage.
- **Spreads 03–14**: Six mini-stories (2 spreads per story). Each story must have its own distinct visual environment (e.g., desert, sea, mountain). Do not repeat environments or actions.
- **Spread 15**: Finale (Static: guide character tucks the child in, "all these stories are yours")

**Crucial constraint for Agent 2**: If the user requests a Bible book, select 6 distinct biblical stories that fit a gentle bedtime tone. If the user requests a different anthology (e.g., "Fairy Tales" or "Animal Fables"), invent 6 unique, distinct stories. Ensure maximum visual variety across the 6 stories.

---

## Text Pattern Per Story (2-spread format)

**Spread A — The story:**
```
[4–6 lines narrating the story, rhyming AABB]
[No {Name} here — this is the story itself]
```

**Spread B — The moral:**
```
[2 lines: what this means for {Name}]
[{Name} appears prominently]
[Ends with a direct address to the child]
```

Example (Daniel and the Lions):
```
SPREAD A:
In a cave full of lions, so quiet and deep,
God stayed close to Daniel and guarded his sleep.
He sent down an angel to keep him from harm,
To hold him in peace and in God's loving arm.

SPREAD B:
Rest softly, dear {Name}, and close your sweet eyes,
For God watches over you from star-filled blue skies.
```

---

## Guide Character

The guide character connects all stories. They appear on Spreads 02 and 15, and optionally
as a small visual element (corner, border) on story spreads.

Recommended: a small animal (white rabbit, little owl, small fox) carrying a book or lantern.
They should feel like a wise, cozy companion — not a hero, just a presence.

---

## Visual Notes

- Each story has its own visual environment (desert, stable, cave, sea)
- The {Name} character (human baby archetype) appears only on moral spreads (Spread B of each story)
- Biblical figures are rendered simply, gently — not photorealistic or overly dramatic
- Consistent soft watercolor style throughout despite varied environments
- Guide character is always small and non-distracting
