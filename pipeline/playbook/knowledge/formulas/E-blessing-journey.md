# Formula E — Blessing Journey (Covenant Book)

## Concept

This book is a ceremony. It is given at baptism, naming day, or christening.
Each spread gifts the child ONE blessing, promise, or virtue.
The parent speaks these aloud as acts of intention, not just as stories.

The emotional core: *"On this day, we give you these gifts. They are yours forever, {Name}."*

---

## Best For

- Baptism gifts
- Naming Day / Christening gifts
- Godparent gifts
- "Blessing book" listings
- Any occasion that is a rite of passage

---

## Tone

Solemn but warm. Ceremonial but not cold. Like wedding vows — emotional, intentional, forever.
The parent or godparent reading this aloud should feel like they are making a promise.
This is the formula most likely to make the buyer cry. That is the goal.

---

## How It Differs from Formulas B and D

- **Formula B** explores ONE feeling through many images (love × waves, love × sunrise...)
- **Formula E** gifts MANY things through one book (courage, wisdom, joy, peace, wonder...)
- **Formula D** tells stories and draws morals — the child receives lessons
- **Formula E** does not tell stories — the child receives promises directly

---

## Narrative Arc (Spreads 01–15 + Afterword)

Do not use a hardcoded list of blessings. Instead, select or invent 12 distinct blessings, promises, or virtues that fit the tone of the occasion (e.g., courage, wonder, kindness, peace).

- **Spread 01**: Dedication (Static: occasion-specific)
- **Spread 02**: Opening: the child arrives into the world, welcomed.
- **Spreads 03–14**: The 12 Blessings. Each spread gifts one specific virtue. Invent these based on the listing. Ensure visual variety: do not use the same setting or action for multiple blessings. Move from active outward virtues (courage, joy) to quiet inward ones (peace, faith, hope).
- **Spread 15**: Finale (Static: "All these gifts are yours, {Name}. Now and always.")

**Crucial constraint for Agent 2**: You must invent the specific blessings and their corresponding visual scenes. Ensure maximum visual variety across the 12 blessings to avoid stagnation.

---

## Text Pattern Per Spread

Each spread has a clear, declarative structure:

```
[HEADER — the blessing name, large: e.g., "COURAGE"]

[Verse — 4 lines]
[Line 1: what this blessing is / looks like]
[Line 2: when the child will need it]
[Line 3: the promise of it]
[Line 4: {Name} receives it]
```

Example (Courage):
```
COURAGE

For the days the world feels big,
when the path is hard and new —
we give you courage, {Name},
it was always meant for you.
```

Example (Wonder):
```
WONDER

May you always stop to look
at the small and overlooked.
The world is full of gifts, {Name},
for those who take a second look.
```

---

## Opening and Finale

**Spread 02 (Opening) — the child is welcomed:**
```
On the day you came to us,
the whole world grew more bright.
We looked at you, dear {Name}, and knew —
everything would be alright.
```

**Spread 15 (Finale) — all blessings given:**
```
And so, dear {Name}, on this day
we gave these gifts to you.
They are not things you have to earn —
they're simply, always true.

Go gently, bravely, joyfully.
The world is waiting, wide.
And in whatever comes ahead,
we'll always be your guide.
```

---

## Visual Notes

- Each blessing spread: the child shown in context of the virtue (e.g., Courage = child taking a first step; Wonder = child looking at a butterfly)
- Use consistent warm, sacred palette: golds, soft whites, gentle blues
- Nature as background, not as the subject (unlike Formula B)
- The child is always present — this book is always about them
- Avoid overtly religious imagery unless listing explicitly religious (cross, bible, church) — blessings can be secular
- For explicitly Christian listings: each blessing can reference a short scripture quote as subtitle
