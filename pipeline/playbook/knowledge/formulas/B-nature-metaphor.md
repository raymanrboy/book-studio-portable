# Formula B — Nature Metaphor

## Concept

The book explores ONE big feeling (love, safety, wonder) through a series of nature images.
Each spread = one natural element as a metaphor for that feeling.
There is no narrative arc — the book is more like a poem collection than a story.

The emotional core: *"You are surrounded by love everywhere you look."*

---

## Best For

- Baptism gifts (spiritual love version)
- Bedtime books ("lullaby" style)
- General love / "I love you because..." books

---

## Tone

Meditative, gentle, slightly lyrical. The parent reads slowly, quietly.
Each spread should feel like a held breath before sleep.

Text should be plainspoken but crafted: concrete image, gentle motion/sound, then a warm promise
to `{Name}`. Avoid vague inspirational filler. The best lines feel inevitable when read aloud.

---

## Narrative Arc (Spreads 01–15 + Afterword)

Do not use a hardcoded list of scenes. Instead, invent unique nature elements and metaphors that fit the specific listing or theme. The nature elements should form a cohesive arc.

- **Spread 01**: Dedication (Static)
- **Spreads 02–07 (The Opening & Building)**: Introduce expansive, welcoming nature images (e.g., morning light, open ocean, gentle rain, a sturdy mountain). Build a feeling of everywhere-present love.
- **Spreads 08–12 (Quiet Focus & Shelter)**: Transition to smaller, more protective, or intimate nature images (e.g., a sheltering tree, quiet bugs, dusk falling, a watchful deer). 
- **Spreads 13–14 (The Close/Bedtime)**: Bring the imagery into absolute stillness (e.g., a moonlit pond, stars, the child falling asleep). Save explicit sleep/bedtime language for these spreads.
- **Spread 15**: Afterword/Finale (Static: "And through it all, {Name}, love is yours.")

**Theme variant: Bedtime arc**
For bedtime listings, order the invented images like a day closing toward sleep (Morning -> Afternoon -> Dusk -> Night). This gives the book a felt progression without turning it into a plot.

**Crucial constraint for Agent 2**: You must invent your own specific nature scenes that fit the exact theme of the book. Ensure a visual progression so the book does not feel like a static collection of random pictures.
---

## Text Pattern Per Spread

Each spread has two parts:

```
[HEADER — large display text, e.g.: "Love is like the waves."]

[VERSE — 4 lines]
[Nature element described],
[What it does / how it moves],
[How it connects to love for {Name}],
[Childward turn to {Name} or "you" where it naturally lands]
```

Example (Ocean spread):
```
Love moves like the waves.

God loves {Name}
like waves that glide —
they always come back,
again each tide.
```

The header can be skipped for secular versions or replaced with a simpler line.
Do not force `{Name}` into the final line if the stronger emotional hinge is line 2 or 3.

## Text Quality Lessons

Best target:
```
God's love wakes the morning
with honey-colored light.
A little bee hums low and slow,
and everything feels right.
```

```
Mountains stand with patient hearts,
steady, calm, and tall.
God's love is strong beneath your feet, {Name},
and holds you when you fall.
```

Use these as the quality bar: simple image, soft mouthfeel, clear promise, natural rhyme.

Avoid:
- repeating flowers or stars across many spreads just because they are pretty
- disconnected image/promise pairs
- too many sleep/rest/nest lines before the final bedtime spreads
- inserting `{Name}` where it breaks the strongest rhythm

---

## Pacing Notes

- For general love books: Spreads 02–08 build through nature images, each standalone
- For bedtime books: prefer morning → day → evening → night → sleep
- Spread 09–12: shift slightly warmer, more "personal" images (shelter, home, light, watching)
- Spread 13–14: bring the child into stillness; save explicit sleep language for the end
- Finale: close the loop — all these things are yours

---

## Visual Notes

- Nature scenes should feel like illustrations, not photographs
- The child appears in some spreads (4–6 of the 14 story spreads) but is not always present
- When child is absent: nature image carries the spread alone
- When child is present: show them interacting with the element (touching a flower, watching waves from shore)
- Consistent soft light throughout — golden hour or early morning palette
